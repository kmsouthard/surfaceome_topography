Cell surface topography

Estimated heights of human cell-surface proteins, the surfaces they make on a cell, and the gaps they hold where two cells meet.

Read the heights as upper bounds. Each assumes its ectodomain is fully extended along the membrane normal, so gaps and exclusion are overstated for proteins that bend or tilt. Contacts assume every pair binds equally well.

Sources and attribution
Heights
Estimated by this work from UniProt sequences and topology, AlphaFold DB models (Jumper et al. 2021; Varadi et al. 2024), Pfam domains, MobiDB disorder and glycosites from UniProt, GlyGen and GlycoMine.
Protein annotations
Location and transmembrane count: UniProt. Surface call: SURFY (Bausch-Fluck et al. 2018, PMID 30373828). Functional class (receptor, transporter, enzyme, miscellaneous): Almén et al. 2009 (PMID 19678920), as carried in the SURFY table. CD numbers: the SURFY table.
Protein drawings
AlphaFold models outlined with CellScape (Silvestre-Ryan, Fletcher and Holmes 2022, bioRxiv).
Cell surfaces
Protein abundances from Expression Atlas E-PROT-1 (Kim et al. 2014, PMID 24870542) and E-PROT-27, and monocyte subsets from Ravenhill et al. 2020 (PMID 32165698).
Interactions
Candidate pairs from STRING and CellphoneDB; which bind across two cells is annotated by this work, each call with its citation in the repository.
Antibody epitopes
Read from structures in the PDB, or from the mapping paper cited beside each antibody.
Rules
Exclusion of a protein about 5 nm taller than a gap: Schmid et al. 2016 (PMID 27980602). The 10 nm phagocytosis range: Bakalar et al. 2018 (PMID 29958103). Kinetic segregation of CD45 and CD148: Davis and van der Merwe 2006 (PMID 16855606); Cordoba et al. 2013 (PMID 23580664).

The Fletcher Lab, Department of Bioengineering, University of California, Berkeley (fletchlab.berkeley.edu).
